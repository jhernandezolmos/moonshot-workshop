Strengths, pain points & opportunities

Management view of what to protect, fix and scale.

Strengths

High baseline adoption. 78.6% are frequent users.

Strong appetite for agents. 77.1% rate the Master Agent 7–10.

Existing AI use is already embedded in core work. Search, drafting, summarization and analysis are mainstream use cases.

Pain points / risks

Trust. Output quality is the dominant concern.

Governance ambiguity. 27.9% either use non-approved tools or are unsure.

Uneven readiness. 38.6% fall into developing or low-engagement clusters.

Opportunities

Trusted search & knowledge. Solves a large repetitive pain point.

Customer / opportunity intelligence. Highest explicit agent demand.

Role-based enablement. Combine training, pre-set agents, peer examples and approved-tool access.

Executive conclusion

What respondents are broadly telling us: “We already use AI, we see value in it, and we want more — but we need it to be trustworthy, approved, easy to use and connected to the information and workflows that matter.”

Management attention should focus on five issues: output reliability, approved-tool clarity, trusted knowledge access, role-based enablement, and disciplined prioritization of the agent roadmap.

Recommended project direction: start with two high-demand, governable MVPs — a Trusted Knowledge/Search Agent and a Customer Intelligence Agent — while simultaneously establishing the quality/governance playbook and practical training needed for scale.

Data quality & methodology

Dataset: 140 responses, one worksheet, 24 fields. IDs are unique. No exact duplicate answer vectors were found.

Missingness: Name and last-modified fields are blank for all respondents. The conditional “If yes, which ones?” field is blank for 113/140; final optional agent suggestion is blank for 86/140.

Response quality: Completion time ranged from 1.7 to 62.6 minutes (median 4.6). The single response under 2 minutes was flagged but not removed.

Cleaning: Non-breaking spaces, invisible formatting markers and obvious casing variants were normalized for analysis. Raw data was preserved separately in the analysis workbook.

Clusters: Standardized K-means solutions (k=2–6) were tested on adoption/readiness features. Weak separation led to a transparent rule-based segmentation. No statistical significance is claimed.

Limitations: Self-reported behavior; uneven regional sample sizes; most respondents have >15 years of experience (107/140), so the sample should not be treated as a balanced workforce census.
